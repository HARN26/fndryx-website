Put one screen in front of three investors and ask them the same question.

The screen is a FNDRYx cohort dashboard: 29 real regional founders, each read on the same instrument — 26 questions across five dimensions (Financial, Team, Market, Business Model, and Funding Strategy), scored deterministically, so the same inputs always produce the same result. Same rows. Same scores. One shared read.

In the three seats: an angel, a venture partner, and a non-dilutive lender — private credit, revenue-based financing, grants, and tax credits. Each is looking at the identical data. And each is looking at a different company, apparently, because each is reading for a different thing.

Here is what each one sees.

## The angel

The angel writes early and small, on conviction about team and market, before there's a model worth underwriting.

> "You're showing me the two dimensions I actually bet on — and this cohort is strongest in exactly those two."

Ask where each founder is *most* ready and the answer is Market for 10 of them and Team for 10 — the two things an angel backs before a financial model exists. That Financial is the weakest dimension for 86% of the cohort doesn't move the angel; this early, it's expected — 13 of the 29 are pre-seed. The angel's hunting ground is the 22 founders sitting in <span className="text-fire-400">Ore</span>, the Developing quadrant — the ones a later, larger check would pass on. To the angel, "early" reads as "in time," not "not yet." A small check and a few months of attention is precisely the instrument that fits a strong-market, strong-team, unfinished-model founder.

## The venture partner

Institutional, larger checks, needs a company that can absorb the capital and show a path diligence can stand on.

> "Six of twenty-nine clear my bar today. The financial gap the angel waves past is the one thing I can't write a round on."

On the Forge sort, 4 founders are <span className="text-fire-400">Forged</span> (Investment Ready) and 2 are <span className="text-fire-400">Hot Iron</span> (High Potential) — the six the venture partner engages now. The other 23 aren't rejects; they're a watch-list. And the same 86%-weakest-in-Financial the angel shrugged off is the venture partner's screen: the financial case is the first thing institutional diligence tests, and it is the lowest of the five dimensions for 25 of 29 founders. The 2 Series A and 9 seed companies are where the partner looks for enough shape; the rest wait until the Financial dimension moves. Same founders, read as "not yet."

## The non-dilutive lender

Takes no equity. Cares about revenue, runway, and qualifying activity — not "fundability."

> "The pile the venture partner just benched is my live pipeline. These founders don't need dilution. They need the financial engine that non-dilutive capital is built to fund."

Same 22 in <span className="text-fire-400">Ore</span>, opposite read. The 5 bootstrap founders are a natural fit — building revenue, not raising a priced round. Many of the 13 pre-seed can qualify for a grant or an R&D credit today, before any equity story is ready. And the weakest dimension for 86% of the cohort — Financial — isn't a disqualifier here. It's the opening: a grant, a credit, or a revenue-based line strengthens exactly the dimension the cohort is thinnest on, without pricing a round or taking a point of equity. The Market-and-Team strength the cohort shows across the board is the real business a lender needs to see before it commits. The founder the fund correctly benched is, to this lens, ready right now — for a different kind of capital.

## Three reads, one cohort

Line them up and the same 22 founders are a reject pile to one lens, a hunting ground to a second, and a live pipeline to a third. Same rows. Same deterministic scores. Three economically rational reads, and none of them is wrong.

What's wrong is a founder walking into the wrong one — pitching the venture partner a round the numbers can't yet support, when an angel would have written the check on market conviction, or a grant would have funded the exact gap the partner screened out.

So the read that matters isn't a verdict. **Weakest in Financial, or sitting in Ore, is a coordinate — not a grade.** It tells a founder which capital fits where they are today. An angel and a grant officer will act on a founder a fund is right to pass on. Knowing which conversation to be in is worth more than one more polished deck aimed at the wrong room.

## The optimization

Now widen it past a single founder, because this is where it matters for the ecosystem and not just the deal.

Readiness is a coordinate that moves. The record is longitudinal by construction — the same founder can be read again later on the same instrument — so a partner isn't stuck with a yes-or-no on whoever is in front of them today. The venture partner can keep a not-yet-ready founder in view and re-enter the moment the Financial dimension moves, with exactly the check that fits then. The non-dilutive lender can fund the gap in the meantime. The angel can be the bridge. The founder isn't lost between rounds; they're legible across them, and the right capital arrives when the signal says it should — not when someone happened to make a warm intro.

That is the optimization. Not more capital — better matching of capital-type to founder-state, and the patience to let the match change over time. The substrate for it is already real: **806 scored responses, 410 dimension scores, and 62 readiness reports** across the cohort, built to compound as founders return and get read again.

And it arrives at the right moment. Regional capital is going direct: 60% of family offices plan to shift strategic allocation this year (UBS, 2026); 69% of family-office deals — and 83% of their *startup* deals — now run as club deals or SPVs (PwC); and 61% of 2025 venture dollars went to AI (OECD), thinning the capital that reaches the non-AI regional middle. More capital deciding for itself, with less shared signal to decide on. A scored, auditable read that every one of these lenses can query is exactly the missing piece.

## For the founder

You can't pick the right room until you can see your own coordinate — across all five dimensions, not the one you happen to feel most confident about. That's the read the assessment is built to give: deterministic, auditable, the same signal you and the partner both see. Where you land tells you which of the three seats to walk toward first.
