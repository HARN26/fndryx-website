Carta published its State of Pre-Seed for Q2 2026 against a snapshot dated August 10. The headline finding is about concentration: fewer instruments, more dollars behind each one, an average instrument size at a record $276,000. Q2 2026 recorded **11,546** pre-seed instruments against **14,825** in Q2 2025 — while total cash raised barely moved, **$3.19B** against **$3.22B**.

That is a true reading. It is not the most useful one if you are the founder on the other side of those instruments.

The more useful row is further down, and Carta is one of the few places it gets published: the median count of separate SAFEs or convertible notes inside a *single* pre-priced round, broken out by round size.

- Under $250K — median **2** instruments
- $250K–$499K — median **4**
- $500K–$999K — median **5**
- $1M–$2.4M — median **7**
- $2.5M–$4.9M — median **10**
- $5M+ — median **11**, with the 75th percentile at **26**

A pre-seed round is not a decision. At $1M–$2.4M it is seven decisions, made by seven people who never meet each other, collected across the months it takes to close them.

## What seven actually costs

Each of those seven is a complete cycle. An introduction. A deck. A call. A follow-up. The same five or six diligence questions, asked again by someone who has no way of knowing they have already been answered six times. A yes, or more often a no with no reason attached.

The thing worth noticing is what each cycle leaves behind, and on which side.

The investor leaves with information. A read on the team. A read on the market. A note in a CRM. A position in a pipeline they will still be holding next year. Carta's report is itself the proof of what that side accumulates: **370,000+** convertible instruments across **27,000+** US startups since Q1 2021, structured, retained, and queryable by round size, by instrument, by industry, by state.

The founder leaves with a wire, or with nothing. There is no artifact. The seventh investor starts from exactly where the first one started, because none of what the first six learned is available to them, and none of what the founder did across those six cycles accrued to the founder.

That is not a networking problem. It is a missing record.

## Two things in the Q2 data make it harder

**The rounds are getting smaller at the bottom.** Rounds under $250K were 25% of pre-priced rounds in Q1 2023. In Q2 2026 they were **34%** — the highest in the series. Over the same stretch, the $1M–$2.4M band fell from 25% to **18%**, and $2.5M–$4.9M from 13% to **10%**. More founders are running the cycle for less money, which means running it again sooner. The distribution moved down while the dollars per instrument moved up.

**The paper got less negotiable.** Of post-money SAFEs issued in the first half of 2026, **73%** carry a valuation cap and nothing else. In 2021 that figure was 58%, and 40% of SAFEs carried a cap *plus* a discount; in the first half of 2026 that combination is down to **21%**. Median caps did rise year over year across every round size — the $1M–$2.4M band moved from **$12M** in Q2 2022 to **$18M** in Q2 2026 — but a higher cap on a thinner instrument is not more leverage. It is the same instrument with fewer terms left to argue about.

And these are not casual checks. In $1M–$2.4M rounds, **75%** of the dollars come from checks of $100,000 or more. At $2.5M–$4.9M it is **86%**. Seven decisions means seven real diligence processes, each one carrying a check large enough that the person writing it will do the work.

Median cumulative dilution for a company whose latest round is $1M–$2.4M on post-money SAFEs is **15.5%**. That is what the seven cost in ownership, paid in advance of a priced round that may be a year out.

## The missing artifact

Ask why the seventh evaluation cannot start from the sixth, and the answer is not confidentiality and it is not competition. It is that no standard object exists to hand over.

There is a deck, which is an argument rather than a measurement. There is a conversation, which is not retained by anyone but the investor. There is a warm introduction, which transfers trust but no facts. Every one of the seven re-derives the same ground from scratch because there is nothing to read instead — and re-derivation by seven separate parties, each working from a founder's self-presentation, is close to the most expensive way a market can price something.

It is still there for the eighth conversation, and the twelfth, and the one two years out with someone none of the first seven have met. Screening still belongs to the evaluation layer and to human judgment; what changes is that the judgment starts from something measured instead of from a fresh re-derivation.

## Why this is not evenly distributed

One more figure, because it sets the price of each of those seven conversations.

AI companies took **48.55%** of all US pre-seed dollars in the first half of 2026 — essentially flat against 2025's **49.79%**. The plateau is the news. The level is the standing condition: roughly half of pre-seed capital allocates against a category label before it allocates against anything measured.

And by census region, the Midwest took **4%** of US pre-seed dollars in Q2 2026, down from 5.2% in Q1 2022. Indiana startups raised **$15.2M** in the quarter. Ohio raised **$6.2M**. Wisconsin, $5.7M. Minnesota, $5.6M. California raised **$1.5B** — **52%** of the national total, with Texas at 9% and New York at 8.6%.

If you are building inside the concentration, seven conversations is a formality with a long tail of eighth chances behind it. If you are building outside it, seven is most of what you get, and every one that starts from zero is a cost you do not recover. The founders who most need the prior six evaluations to count are precisely the founders for whom they currently count least.

## The part you control

You cannot change the number of instruments in a pre-seed round. That number is a fact about how this market clears now, and Carta's series suggests it is going up rather than down.

What you can change is whether the seventh conversation starts from zero.

Measure it once, deterministically, on the same five dimensions capital actually reads. Own the result. Add to it as the company moves. The round will still be seven decisions — but they stop being seven unrelated ones.
